Session 1 (6 AM – 8 AM)
Understanding Embeddings
What is an Embedding?

An embedding is a numerical representation of text.

Example:

"Home Loan"

↓

[0.12, 0.55, 0.91, ...]
Why Convert Text into Numbers?

Machines understand numbers, not meaning.

Embeddings capture semantic meaning.

Example

Texts:

Home Loan

Housing Loan

Mortgage Loan

produce vectors that are close together.

Embedding Dimensions

Example:

[0.23, 0.67, 0.11]

3 dimensions.

Real models:

384
768
1024
1536
3072

dimensions.

Similarity Search

Instead of:

Exact Match

we search:

Meaning Match

Example:

Stored:

Home Loan Interest Rate

User asks:

Mortgage Interest

Embeddings help retrieve the right document.

Cosine Similarity

Most common similarity metric.

Formula:

cos(θ)=
∥A∥∥B∥
A⋅B
	​


Interpretation:

1.0  → Exactly Similar
0.8  → Highly Similar
0.5  → Somewhat Similar
0.0  → Unrelated

    SentenceTransformer

Session 3 (4 PM – 6 PM)
Chunking Strategies
Why Chunking?

Suppose PDF contains:

500 Pages

LLMs cannot efficiently process the entire document.

Split into chunks.

Bad Chunking
500 Pages
 ↓
One Chunk
Better
500 Pages
 ↓
1000 Character Chunks
Example
text = """
Home loan policy...
Interest rate...
Eligibility...
"""

chunk_size = 100

chunks = [

    text[i:i+chunk_size]

    for i in range(
        0,
        len(text),
        chunk_size
    )
]
LangChain Chunking
pip install langchain
from langchain.text_splitter import (
    RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter
)

splitter = (
    RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(
        chunk_size=500,
        chunk_overlap=50
    )
)

chunks = splitter.split_text(text)
Why Overlap?

Without overlap:

Chunk 1:
Home loan interest

Chunk 2:
rate is 8%

Context lost.

With overlap:

Chunk 1:
Home loan interest rate

Chunk 2:
interest rate is 8%
